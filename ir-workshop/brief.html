Incident brief

A phishing attack against IROrg. Read this before you start. It is the scenario, the evidence the SOC has gathered, and exactly what your team needs to produce.

ScenarioEvent timeline IndicatorsAffected assets NIST phasesYour objective What to produce

Scenario

IROrg runs a Windows domain that supports core business services, with corporate email and single sign on for internal apps. A central SIEM aggregates endpoint, mail gateway and authentication logs, and endpoint protection runs on every workstation.

Over a short window the service desk receives a cluster of reports about an email impersonating IT support, warning of a security verification deadline and pushing recipients to a linked page to confirm their account. At least one user entered credentials on the page and a second opened an attached document. Soon after, the SOC sees endpoint and network activity.

What the SOC sees
  • Multiple users report a near identical email impersonating IT support
  • A spoofed sign in or verification page on recently registered look alike domains
  • At least one confirmed credential submission to the external page
  • A document attachment executed from a user's Downloads, flagged by endpoint protection
  • Encoded activity and a dropper writing a secondary binary on a workstation
  • Outbound connections from that host to unfamiliar external infrastructure, suspected C2
  • Mail gateway logs showing a burst of inbound mail from a single external sender within minutes
  • SIEM correlation against threat intel feeds returning hits on several observed domains
Impact. Harvested credentials plus an active trojan foothold set up account takeover and lateral movement if unchecked.

The NIST incident handling phases

Record each phase as a case task in TheHive and provide a description of your actions in each based on your findings. Here is what each one means.

1Detection

Confirm the security incident and summarise your view using the incident detection details already provided in the brief.

2Analysis

Investigate and analyse the incident details to understand what happened, its scope, and its impact. Run analysis and summarise your analysis findings in MISP and Cortex here

3Containment

Limit the incident to prevent further spread or damage.

4Eradication

Remove the threat and fix the vulnerabilities that allowed it to occur.

5Recovery

Restore affected systems and services to normal operation and monitor for any recurrence.

6Post Incident Activity

Review what happened, capture lessons learned, and improve future incident response.

Your objective

Respond as IROrg’s internal incident response team using the four tools, following the NIST incident handling phases and recording each phase as a case task in TheHive. The response actions are yours to decide and the incident details are provided above.

  1. Coordinate with your team in Mattermost.
  2. Create a case in TheHive with an appropriate title.
  3. Add six case tasks named after the NIST phases and document your response actions in each.
  4. Add the provided indicators as observables, plus any artifacts you extract.
  5. Record the IoCs in MISP and correlate any new ones you find.
  6. Analyse observables in Cortex (VirusTotal, AbuseIPDB, and others).
  7. When every task is done, close and resolve the case in TheHive.

What to produce

  • A case with an appropriate title.
  • Six case tasks, each titled after a NIST phase, each with a short summary of what your team did.
  • Interesting indicators added as observables, plus any extra artifacts you extract.
